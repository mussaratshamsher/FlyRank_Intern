Rejection Log

Discernment is graded. Here are three AI-generated images I rejected — and the genuine reasoning behind each cut.

❌ Rejected: AI-Generated "Professional Portrait"
Why rejected: The generated portrait looked polished but it wasn't me. This portfolio is about a real person with real skills. Using a fake portrait for a "Software Engineer & AI Developer" portfolio would be immediately spotted by technical recruiters and erodes trust before they even read a single case study. Decision: Real photo of myself — even if less polished — is the only honest choice.
🖼️ [AI-generated portrait — discarded] — "Looks good, isn't me."
❌ Rejected: AI-Generated "App Mockup" for Content Analyzer
Why rejected: The AI-generated mockup showed a sleek, idealized version of a content analysis tool — perfect gradients, no real content, and a UI that looked good but didn't actually work. Since I have a real, deployed Content Analyzer with actual screenshots (Image 3 above), using a mockup would be intentionally misleading. A real screenshot shows real API responses, real loading states, and real design trade-offs. Decision: Real capture of the live Content Analyzer.
🖼️ [AI-generated mockup — discarded] — "Shiny but fake. The real app proves more."
❌ Rejected: AI-Generated "Hero Illustration" (Style Mismatch)
Why rejected: The first generation of the hero background came back as a photorealistic abstract with warm oranges and golds — visually striking, but completely mismatched to the Identity Kit's cool purple/teal palette. I iterated the prompt three times to shift the mood, but the AI kept defaulting to warm sunset tones. The fourth attempt finally produced something usable in the right colours. Lesson: Style consistency requires persistent prompt iteration — it's not "one and done." The rejected versions are a reminder that AI generation needs human direction to stay on-brand.
🖼️ [AI-generated warm-toned hero — discarded] — "Beautiful but off-brand. Needed 4 prompt iterations to fix."
